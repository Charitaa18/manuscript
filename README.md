# Project 1: *Manuscript*

Demo/template for our [first projects](https://typography-interaction-2627.github.io/project/1/).

> **Students will choose a seminal design text from [readings.design](https://readings.design), read and respond to it, and typeset their selection and reply together.**
>
> The goal of this project is to hone your basic skills in typography, focusing on expression, hierarchy, and form appropriate to a work. You will do this through exploration, trial and error, and responding to critical feedback. And then you will execute this typesetting in code, as a web page

<!-- Assignment -->
The assignment was to take a chosen reading and our personal response to it and develop a website using semantically accurate HTML and CSS. I chose Nigel Cross's Designerly Ways of Knowing as my reading.

<!-- Reasoning and thought process -->
The article begins by introducing a third area of education that Cross argues has not been acknowledged. Science and arts & humanities are the two areas traditionally given weight, while design, despite informing how we interact with almost everything in the world, has not been given the same recognition. He refers to this as the “man-made world.”
I chose three different typefaces to reference these three branches of education. Science is represented by the headings in Oswald, an extremely tightly packed sans-serif font. It has a bold, authoritative, and elongated appearance, which reminded me of how I interpret scientific data: more vertical, structured, and condensed.
The main text is set in Montserrat, another sans-serif font that contrasts with Oswald through its wider and more geometric structure. Its readability and legibility represent arts & humanities.
The unrecognised third branch, design, is represented by Kalam. Since Cross refers to this branch as the “man-made world,” I wanted to use this typeface to showcase my own thoughts while keeping them personal and soft to convey emotion. It almost feels like an intruder within the article, visually separating my voice from the original text.
The reason my responses are faded is also connected to the idea of design being overlooked. My opinion, representing this hidden or unrecognised branch, is not given effortless visual weight. It has to be intentionally looked for. The response is not completely hidden, but it is easy to overlook unless the reader interacts with it by hovering over it, at which point it reveals itself.
For the visual look , I tried to match the overall tone of the article. To me, the reading did not create a sense of urgency. Instead, it felt calm and thought-provoking, so I wanted the visuals to reflect that feeling.

<!-- Structure -->
The headings are positioned on one end of the page and the article body on the other, representing the two established sides of education. The response, representing design, acts as the bridge between them, stretching from one side to the other. This reflects Cross's idea that design can be seen as a synthesis of the sciences and humanities.
The way I structured the website was to create a sort of back and forth dialogue between the reading and my response. The reading is extremely insightful but also quite long, so I felt that placing my responses directly below the sections that spoke to me would help the reader understand my interpretation and opinion as they move through the article.
